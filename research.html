Research program

Distinct scholarship for clinical inquiry.

The Clinical Inquiry Ecosystem connects four independent projects without collapsing discovery, integrative model building, and patient-specific practice reasoning into one activity.

A Boyer-informed model

Different questions require different scholarly work.

Boyer’s model distinguishes scholarship by purpose. Here, discovery develops warranted claims, integration combines knowledge into population-level causal models, and practice brings that knowledge into reasoned engagement with an individual situation. Physiolog contributes a separate source of generative-mechanism knowledge.

Working principles

Epistemic safeguards belong in the architecture.

01

Population knowledge is not a patient conclusion.

General evidence constrains individual reasoning; it does not determine it without patient findings, context, and uncertainty.

02

Association is not causation.

Evidence types, causal claims, concepts, measurements, and curation decisions remain explicit rather than being collapsed into one record.

03

AI output is candidate knowledge.

Extraction can assist scientific work, but ontology resolution and human curation remain necessary before a claim enters the knowledge base.

04

Explanations should remain inspectable.

Inference systems should expose evidence, assumptions, model versions, uncertainty, and reasoning steps to scientific and clinical review.

The boundary

One ecosystem, not one monolithic product.

The projects are conceptually aligned, independently owned, and not yet technically integrated.

stats4PT develops methods of discovery, while Physiolog contributes generative-mechanism knowledge. Models4PT performs the distinct integrative work of building population-level causal models. The Clinical Inference Engine uses those models with individual information for practice reasoning; population evidence alone does not determine care.

See project details

The aim is not to automate clinical judgment. It is to make the knowledge, assumptions, uncertainty, and inferential steps supporting that judgment more explicit.
