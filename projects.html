01

stats4PT

A free learning resource about the language and methods of scientific inquiry for physical therapy.

stats4PT examines how observations become evidence, estimates, and warranted scientific claims through statistical inference, uncertainty, Bayesian reasoning, causal reasoning, and critical reflection.

Its eight self-hosted lessons serve physical therapists, educators, students, and researchers. As discovery scholarship, stats4PT contributes candidate evidence and causal knowledge to model building; it does not own comprehensive model integration or patient-specific practice reasoning.

02

Physiolog

A muscle-centered approach to clinical physiology for physical therapy.

Physiolog combines a 12-chapter open textbook with interactive cardiovascular models. It treats circulation, respiration, renal regulation, and extracellular fluid as integrated support systems for muscle function and movement.

The project grew from approximately 30 years of teaching and addresses a practical curriculum gap: medical physiology texts are broader than DPT needs, while exercise physiology texts often lack sufficient clinical context. Its generative-mechanism knowledge makes a distinct contribution to integrative causal model building.

03

Models4PT

Infrastructure for transparent, computable causal knowledge in rehabilitation.

Models4PT owns the integrative work of combining evidence, generative mechanisms, context, provenance, uncertainty, and disagreement into comprehensive population-level causal models. Its safeguards are deliberate: association is not causation, extracted text is not accepted knowledge, and diagrams are views rather than the canonical record.

The current prototype includes tested Python domain objects, conservative mock extraction, an ontology-resolution boundary, and a heart-failure/NMES case study. It is not yet a deployed knowledge platform or clinical product.

04

Clinical Inference Engine

A research initiative for patient-specific, uncertainty-aware clinical reasoning.

The project defines the practice-reasoning layer of the ecosystem: combining population causal knowledge with individual patient information through Bayesian updating, diagnostic hypothesis comparison, temporal reasoning, and inspectable explanations.

At present, this is an architectural and scholarly direction rather than runnable software. It is designed to consume versioned population knowledge from Models4PT without mutating that canonical knowledge or treating population estimates as automatic patient conclusions.
